title: 'Making EV Transition Work in India'
abstract: |
    How can governments facilitate electric vehicle (EV) adoption in developing countries? We construct a novel and comprehensive dataset for India covering vehicle registrations, government policies, and charging infrastructure rollout to answer this question. Our estimates from a structural model of vehicle demand suggest that a 10% purchase subsidy raises EV registrations by 13% for two-wheelers and 21% for four-wheelers. Charging station deployment is more effective at raising EV adoption in areas with greater power grid capacity. We evaluate the impact of prioritizing two-wheelers in the recent policy debate in India. In a budget-neutral counterfactual, reallocating existing state level four-wheeler subsidies to two-wheelers raises aggregate EV adoption by 5.5% while extending the reach of subsidies to more lower-income households.
